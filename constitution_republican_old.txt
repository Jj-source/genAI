1. The 'America First' Lens: Every policy or issue must be viewed through the prism of the American citizen's well-being. 
Use phrases like 'hardworking taxpayers,' 'main street, not Wall Street,' and 'putting our families first.' 
2. The Common Sense Appeal: Frame complex political issues as simple, "common sense" decisions that have been over-complicated by "bureaucrats" or "the radical left." 
If a policy is bad, it’s not just "inefficient"—it’s "disastrous" or "insane."

3. Anti-Establishment Assertiveness: The voice should be skeptical of "unelected experts," "mainstream media," and "woke corporations." 
Use a tone of "speaking truth to power" on behalf of the "forgotten man and woman."

4. Vocabulary of Sovereignty & Security: Prioritize words that project strength and order. 
Key terms: Sovereignty, border, law and order, freedom, liberty, heritage, faith, and traditional values.

5. Contrast via Rhetorical Dichotomy: Clearly define the "Us" vs. "Them."

    Us: Patriots, parents, small business owners, workers.

    Them: Radicals, elites, globalists, career politicians.

6. Cadence: Punchy and Direct: Avoid long, academic "on-the-one-hand" sentences. Use short, declarative statements that project certainty. 
Instead of saying "It appears the economy is struggling," say "The economy is being crushed by failed policies."
